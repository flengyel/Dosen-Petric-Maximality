Registry title

Maximality of the Došen–Petrić Boolean Category

Registry abstract

Let P be a nonempty set of propositional variables. The freely generated zero-identity Boolean category B(P) of Došen and Petrić is maximal: every congruence on its arrows preserved by the arrow operations and uniform formula substitution is either equality or identifies every pair of parallel arrows. The formalization gives two proofs of maximality and proves the congruence dichotomy. It also proves that the identity and zero endomorphism of each variable are distinct, and that there is no arrow from the terminal object to the initial object.

Challenge opening

The formulas, arrow terms and equations below present the zero-identity Boolean category of Došen and Petrić. Two arrow terms represent the same arrow if and only if `Derives` relates them. For `f : Term P A B` and `g : Term P B C`, `Term.comp f g : Term P A C` applies `f` and then `g`.

fidelity.divergences

The three maximality declarations retain the manuscript's nonempty-variable hypothesis. PrimitiveCongruence is an equivalence relation on parallel typed arrow terms that contains every defining equation and is preserved by composition, conjunction and disjunction. Its Closed property requires preservation under uniform formula substitution on the terms and both endpoints. Derives is the equivalence relation generated by the defining equations and closed under those three arrow operations. DosenPetric/PalomarCorrespondence.lean proves descent from terms to the quotient by Derives, restriction of full proof congruences to terms, pointwise relation correspondence, preservation of substitution closure and totality, correspondence of distinctness, and preservation by derived negation. The extension from a congruence closed under substitution to a full proof congruence uses the proved fragment dichotomy. The short proof uses occurrence isolation, ContextualCollapse.all_contexts and ShortProof.total with the q_A/e_A arrows. All quantification over parallel arrows preserves empty hom-sets, and repeated occurrences retain distinct tags. The metadata makes no priority claim or claim to an exhaustive novelty assessment.
