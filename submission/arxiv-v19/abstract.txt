Categorical proof theory expresses identifications of proofs as
equations between arrows. We prove that the zero-identity Boolean
category of Do\v{s}en and Petri\'c, freely generated by a nonempty set
of propositional variables, is maximal. Adjoining an equation between
distinct arrows with the same source and target, and closing under
the arrow operations and uniform formula substitution, identifies
all parallel arrows. This disproves their conjecture in Section~14.2
of the September 2007 revision of \emph{Proof-Theoretical Coherence}.
The proof uses Boolean Coherence to select a pair of variable
occurrences on which the two arrows differ. Composition with arrows
selecting these occurrences, uniform substitution and two
isomorphisms yield equality of the product projections. We also
prove that any zero-identity Boolean category satisfying such an
additional equation under every interpretation of the variables has
at most one arrow between any two objects. The Lean formalization
proves coherence, maximality and this model corollary for the category
presented by the defining equations. It also establishes the
correspondence between substitution on tagged occurrence sets and
substitution on finite ordinals.
